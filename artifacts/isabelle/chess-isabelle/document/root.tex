\title{Chess in Isabelle/HOL: A Verified Executable Semantics}
\author{Arthur Ramos}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We formalize an executable semantics of orthodox chess in Isabelle/HOL.
Finite board coordinates, geometric attacks, special moves, deterministic
state transitions, check and legal moves are connected to an exhaustive
finite reference generator by proved soundness and completeness theorems.
This is a verified correctness kernel, not a Stockfish-style performance
generator.  The entry
also provides histories, repetition and clock predicates, textual and typed
FEN (with an exact textual placement round trip) and coordinate/SAN notation
interfaces, history-aware bounded forced-mate search with an explicit terminal
and defender-claim policy, intended-move draw claims,
history-aware recursive certificate soundness and arbitrary-depth certificate
completeness,
reflection involutions with attack/ray, pseudo-legal and legal-move transport,
unique-king check transport, checkmate/stalemate reflection, perft,
and executable regression facts, including the standard initial-position,
Kiwipete, and en-passant/promotion-rich perft suites.  The SAN layer proves
unconditional injectivity of canonical printing on legal moves and the
corresponding parse-after-print theorem.  Its structured proof covers
destination and capture fields, minimal disambiguation, pawn geometry,
promotion, en passant, and castling; for non-pawn normal moves, equal
non-castling SAN text is explicitly decomposed into destination, capture,
disambiguation, and source equality.  The finite-list uniqueness predicate is
retained as an executable regression certificate for concrete positions.  The
semantic mate policy includes the exact unbounded reflexive-transitive
dead-position predicate, while the executable policy leaves the terminal
predicate to its caller.  The textual FEN parser
is proved to recover
the printed FEN on every position with a positive fullmove number; this is a
parse-after-print canonicalization theorem, not a converse for arbitrary
non-canonical text.  A separate showcase theory
checks a three-ply certificate for a conventional two-move mate-in-two,
including an exhaustive split over the defender's legal replies; the
history-threaded certificate checks the same witness under the claim-aware
mate policy.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
The formalization follows the orthodox rules baseline in the FIDE Laws of
Chess \cite{FIDE2023Laws}.  Its interchange layers use six-field Forsyth--Edwards
Notation and the Universal Chess Interface \cite{ForsythEdwards,UCIProtocol},
while the executable perft regression follows the standard move-generator
validation convention \cite{ChessProgrammingPerft}.  The development keeps
these interoperability conventions separate from the semantic legal-move
relation and makes the declarative specification/refinement boundary explicit.
The generated move list is intentionally an exhaustive finite reference
implementation: it enumerates the complete candidate universe and filters it
through the independently stated legality relation.  Its exact refinement
theorem is therefore a correctness result, not a claim of engine-grade move
generation performance.  The structural \texttt{position\_invariant} is likewise not
full chess legality: \texttt{legal\_position} is reachability from the initial
position, and reachable positions are proved to satisfy the invariant.

\section*{Related Work}
The proof-engineering style follows the executable semantic specifications and
refinement patterns presented by Nipkow and Klein \cite{NipkowKlein2014}.
Formal chess work spans several complementary scopes.  Hurd formalized chess
rules in HOL4 and verified the construction of pawnless endgame databases
\cite{Hurd2005ChessEndgames}; Mari\'c, Jani\v{c}i\'c, and Malikovi\'c verified an
executable KRK strategy in Isabelle/HOL with Z3 \cite{MaricJanicicMalikovic2015};
and Malikovi\'c developed a Coq framework for automated retrograde-chess
reasoning \cite{Malikovic2008Retrograde}.  Those projects emphasize verified
endgame reasoning or retrograde analysis.  The present entry instead gives a
declarative orthodox move semantics with all major special moves, an
exhaustive finite-generator refinement, history and FIDE draw policies,
notation interfaces, symmetry transport, bounded mate certificates, and
executable perft regressions for a general reference kernel.
